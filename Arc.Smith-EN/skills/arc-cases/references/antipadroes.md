# Anti-patterns and warning signs

## Pitfalls to Avoid in Discovery

To keep the quality of the information gathered:

- **Do not skip steps by suggesting solutions too early:** keep the focus on understanding the problem first. If you introduce solutions during the interviews, you may bias the answers.
- **Do not take statements as facts without validation:** whenever possible, ask to see evidence or examples (reports, emails, systems) of what is being described. This confirms you really understood it correctly.
- **Do not let unknown terms go unclarified:** if jargon or acronyms come up, ask for an explanation. It is better to ask than to assume something wrong.
- **Do not listen to only one source:** avoid forming your conclusion based on a single interviewee, especially if the process involves several roles. This can lead to a distorted or incomplete view of reality.

## Common Mistakes When Documenting the AS-IS

When recording the current process, avoid some pitfalls:

- **Over-generalizing:** do not describe the steps vaguely. Be specific (e.g., *"The purchasing analyst registers the order in System X"* is better than *"The order is processed"*).
- **Relying only on formal documentation:** the real procedure often differs from the official one. Prefer the users' account of how the task really happens.
- **Ignoring difficulties or failures:** also document the problems and workarounds of the current process — they often point exactly to where AI will be most useful.
- **Not validating the description:** whenever possible, share the AS-IS draft with the interviewees or colleagues to make sure there were no significant mistakes or omissions.

## Anti-patterns in the Delivery Phase

Avoid practices that undermine the final impact:

- **Insufficient or confusing documentation:** not delivering enough technical or usage details can lead to the solution being abandoned. On the other hand, too many irrelevant details can discourage readers. Balance it, focusing on what is needed to understand and operate the solution.
- **Overly technical language in the presentation:** when showing results, adapt the language to the audience. Highlight business gains and practical improvements instead of going deep into the AI model's technical jargon.
- **Ignoring the change factor:** do not underestimate the human challenge of adopting something new. If you notice hesitation or lack of engagement, plan extra training sessions or follow the first uses to make sure the solution gains traction.
- **Lack of next steps:** delivering and leaving without pointing a way forward can leave the solution stalled. Always recommend how it can be expanded or maintained.

## Warning Signs in AI Projects

Watch for early indicators of problems throughout the process:

- **Low participant engagement:** if participation in interviews or tests is weak, it may be necessary to re-engage those involved by explaining the importance of the project, or to seek leadership support to mobilize the team.
- **Misaligned goals:** if stakeholders have very different views about the project's direction, it is essential to realign (sometimes by recapping the central problem and the goals).
- **Excessive haste or unrealistic expectations:** if someone expects miraculous results without following the steps, consider explaining the prototype's scope again and possibly adjusting deadlines or goals.
- **Essential resources unavailable:** if during the build you find that a crucial piece of data or access to a system is missing, evaluate alternatives (simulated data, or changing the case). This kind of blocker must not be ignored — handle it transparently and propose solutions or escalations to resolve it.
- **No "owner" for the solution:** if it is not clear who will be responsible for the solution after delivery, identify an owner before closing the project, ensuring the initiative will continue.
