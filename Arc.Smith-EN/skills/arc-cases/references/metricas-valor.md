# Value metrics

## Value Metrics

It is essential to demonstrate the measurable impact of the solution:

- **Time:** how much manual work time was saved? E.g.: *"activity X went from 3 hours to 15 minutes, a 90% saving."*
- **Speed:** if a process that took several days is now instant or considerably faster, make it evident.
- **Quality:** reductions in error rates, increased consistency or reliability of results. E.g.: *"80% reduction in manual entry errors."*
- **Increased capacity:** what was not possible before (due to human limits of time or cognition) is now feasible. E.g.: *"100% of documents are now analyzed (before, only a 20% sample was checked)."*

In the context of a prototype, solid data is not always available for every metric, so use realistic estimates:

- Use information provided by the interviewees to extrapolate gains (for example: *"before, each requester spent ~30 minutes, so the solution saves about 10 hours a week across the whole team"*).
- If there are no exact numbers, present potential gains qualitatively (*"significant reduction in response time"*, *"process standardization and consequent quality improvement"*).

## Balance between Quantitative and Qualitative Metrics

Not every benefit is easy to quantify, so balance:

- **Quantitative indicators:** time, volume, cost, percentages, etc., to give concreteness and credibility to the results.
- **Qualitative benefits:** positive user testimonials about ease of use or satisfaction, better team climate, strategic gain potential (such as freeing the team to focus on innovation).

A mix of both types of evidence makes the argument richer and more convincing.
